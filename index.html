How a session works

A short placement finds your floor so a working player is not stuck on octaves. Each room is one focused session, about five minutes. You stay until it is clean. Then the next room opens.

Hints are allowed. Spot me holds the tonic under the prompt. Break it down plays the notes apart. Both cost score; neither is locked behind the purchase. The same gym runs from the first fifth to altered dominants.

Piano and guitar, recorded, not synthesized as a square wave

Ear training is only as good as the instrument you hear. EarPT plays a sampled concert grand, one recording per key, and a microphone-recorded acoustic guitar. Switch in Settings when you want strings instead of hammers.

Questions

What is ear training?

Ear training is learning to recognize pitches, intervals, chords, and rhythm by ear. It is how you close the gap between a line you hear in your head and the same line on an instrument.

What is the best ear training app for iPhone?

There isn't one best ear training app. EarPT is for musicians who want a short daily session on iPhone or iPad, on sampled piano or guitar, without a subscription.

Is EarPT a subscription?

No. The app is free to download. Ten workout sessions are free after placement. Unlocking the rest of the gym is a one-time in-app purchase (about $9.99 / 129 kr). Not a subscription.

How is EarPT different from Functional Ear Trainer?

Functional Ear Trainer taught a generation to hear intervals against a tonic. EarPT starts there, then adds chords, melody dictation, and progressions in the same gym, on sampled piano or guitar, as a daily session on iPhone.

Does EarPT work for beginners and for professional musicians?

Yes. Placement sets the floor. A beginner can start on fifths and major versus minor. A working player is not sent back to octaves. The top of the gym includes sevenths, inversions, longer phrases, and altered dominants.

How do you train intervals, chords, and melody in EarPT?

Intervals: two notes in a key, you name the distance. Chords: you hear a voicing and name the quality. Melody: you hear a short phrase and place it on the staff. Progressions use roman numerals in the key you are in.

Does EarPT use real piano and guitar sounds?

Yes. A sampled grand piano (one sample per key) and a microphone-recorded acoustic guitar. Not a square-wave synth. You pick the instrument in Settings.

Is EarPT on iPhone and iPad? What languages?

Yes. iPhone and iPad, iOS 17 and later. English, Swedish, German, Spanish, and French. Download it on the App Store.
